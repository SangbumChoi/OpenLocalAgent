                                                         Abstract

1              Tool-calling research centres on large language models, leaving models in the tens
2              of millions of parameters underexamined. We give an end-to-end recipe for a 10
3              to 95M parameter caller, spanning pretraining through episode supervision, and
4              price its ingredients against nineteen released checkpoints under one harness on
5              ten public evaluation suites, including explicitly labelled text-only, ﬁltered and
6              ﬁrst-action projections. Data and additional pretraining compute transfer selec-
7              tively: an imported split improves predominantly the suites whose tool inventory
8              it shares, and longer pretraining carries never-trained suites past chance. Under
9              the maps tested, coordinate-preserving projections from larger models do not im-
10             prove suite scores over random initialisation, though same-shape weights transfer
11             strongly. Single-call accuracy does not yield task completion in our simulator:
12             all single-call-trained checkpoints complete zero multi-step episodes. Episode su-
13             pervision at a small mixture share lifts completion from zero to double-digit rates
14             with no consistent single-call cost across the two scope groups, and an oracle de-
15             composition identiﬁes argument ﬁdelity, rather than tool ordering, as the larger
16             remaining bottleneck in this simulator.




17   1 Introduction

18   Research on tool-calling agents advances on the back of large language models, and open releases
19   follow it: general families commonly bottom out near 0.6B [9,10], the smallest general instruction
20   baseline here is 135M [8], and the 45M needle2 [37] is a rare specialist. We give a controlled,
21   stage-by-stage study of catalog-conditioned models from 10 to 95M parameters.
22   A custom edge-sized architecture forfeits compatible released weights. We ask how catalog calling
23   and multi-step completion are trained into it, and what each recipe ingredient is worth.
24   Our 10.5M/43M/95.3M family is the instrument. One contract, parser and chance-ﬂoor harness
25   scores nineteen released checkpoints on ten public evaluation suites, with projections labelled; con-
26   trolled comparisons use matched ﬁne-tuning and shipped rows are separate. We also report four-
27   thread server-CPU decode.
28   Three ﬁndings organise the paper: data transfers mainly to covered inventories and more pretraining
29   carries unseen suites past chance; tested cross-shape projections fail although same-shape weights
30   transfer (§4); and single-call checkpoints complete no simulator episodes until episode supervision
31   lifts completion to 17.6–34.3%, including open-model controls (§5, App. E).

32   2 Related work
33   Small agents. Toolformer [52], ReAct [53] and Gorilla [54] establish tool use; Belcak et al. [14]
34   argue that repetitive schema-bound calls suit smaller models, but study 1B–7B. Shen et al. [15] and
35   Żywot et al. [16] recover weak callers by adding planner/caller specialists or multi-agent orchestra-
36   tion at inference. We instead ask what one 10M–95M policy learns from each training stage, without
37   an inference-time ensemble.
38   Edge callers. TinyAgent [13] retrieves tools for 1.1B/7B models; MobileLLM [26] designs sub-
39   billion architectures; Octopus v2 [27] binds a ﬁxed pool to tokens; Hammer [28] masks irrelevant
40   functions. Our models and needle2 [37] instead accept runtime descriptions, but needle2 retrieves
41   ﬁve and constrains decoding. Thus ﬁxed-token systems buy latency by compiling the inventory into
42   weights, retrieval systems shorten the prompt, and our experiment isolates free generation from a
43   prompt-supplied, changing catalog.
44   Multi-step use. BUTTON [55] and PARL-MT [56] train multi-turn composition and progress; tau-
45   bench [57] and ToolSandbox [39] evaluate stateful completion. We test whether sparse episode
46   supervision creates completion at 10M–95M, whether single-call accuracy transfers without it, and
47   what the added supervision costs on the original call suites.
48   Weight reuse. Net2Net [18], bert2BERT [19], LiGO [20], HyperCloning [21] and Weight Sub-
49   cloning [22] transfer across sizes; Wiring Beats Blending [58] shows map structure matters. The
50   former methods preserve functions or learn mappings; our deliberately cheaper test asks whether
51   coordinate-preserving cross-family slices can replace pretraining, then uses same-shape adoption as
52   a positive control. We also test whether ﬁne-tuning update magnitude predicts reusable regions; Yu
53   et al. [46] instead localise individual super weights (§4, App. B).

93    4 Data, weights, or neither: the intervention ledger

94    With the baseline tradeoffs established, each intervention in Table 2 runs against the control isolating
95    it, chain and hyper-parameters ﬁxed. Terms: adoption copies weights between identical shapes,
96    projection crosses a shape change; union arms train on the union corpus, wide adds xLAM’s split,
97    +teacher relabels it.
98    Generic backbones as a control for purpose pretraining. We compare the generic block with
99    the matched-stage LocalAgent-96M rescore (App. B), not its stronger ﬁve-source shipped row, and
100   report dataset-author / study-projection descriptive means, never one combined rank. Pythia scales
101   from 13.2/17.6 (70M) through 38.2/36.4 (160M) to 88.7/54.4 (410M); LocalAgent-96M reaches
102   39.3/45.6, level with Pythia-160M on the author splits, above it on the projections, and below
103   Pythia-410M on both. The result is not a universal purpose-pretraining advantage: DistilGPT-2-
104   82M reaches 53.4/47.8. Against Pythia-160M, only ToolBench clears the replicate spread (+8.2),
105   and LocalAgent’s 23.6 remains near its 22.9% chance ﬂoor; ToolACE (−4.4), xLAM (+1.3), BFCL
106   (+2.0) and AndroidControl (+11.9) remain inside their spreads, while Mind2Web is majority-ﬂoor
107   dominated. The shipped 96M’s 76.8/43.5 is unmatched and its gains concentrate in inventories
108   added by ﬁve-source SFT, analysed below. Thus the per-suite evidence places the 96M near Pythia-
109   160M and below Pythia-410M; corpus, tokenizer, architecture and budget still co-vary.
110   Covered-inventory data transfers; the tested cross-shape projections do not. Adding one open
111   split (xLAM’s own, 18,000 rows after a prompt-hash check against the evaluation shards) moves
112   xLAM 26.7 points, 3.4× its spread, from below chance-in-catalog to well above it. Relabelling that
113   corpus with the strongest ﬁne-tuned teacher (sequence-level distillation [23], the rung that remains
114   when vocabularies differ [24]), keeping the teacher’s call only where it agrees with the gold, recov-
115   ers the device-control points widening cost, inside their spreads, so suggestive only. Projecting a
116   differently-shaped open checkpoint into our model clears nothing on any open-catalog suite under
117   the coordinate-preserving maps we test; a learned growth operator in the LiGO style [20] is the
118   untested alternative.
119   The dominant measured mechanism is inventory coverage, not broad catalog skill. xLAM rises
120   26.7 while ToolACE, equally open-catalog, does not move: 99.4% of xLAM’s evaluation tool names
121   occur in the imported split, against 1.0% of ToolACE’s, and the same import moves ToolBench,
122   0.0% of whose gold tools it covers, from 12.4 to 11.5, i.e. nowhere. The import primarily teaches
123   the inventory the evaluation will ask about, rather than a general method for reading an unfamiliar
124   one, though the resulting behaviour is not mere lookup: renaming its tools costs the 96M rung
125   under four points and the 11M rung thirty-two (App. B). Hammer [28] documents this name-keyed
126   shortcutting [17]; the audit turns it into a number to compute before training, the evaluation set’s
127   tool-name coverage of any imported split. What data import never bought, longer pretraining did:
128   the same 465M-token corpus run for 4.5 epochs (16,000 steps against 1,599, 3.3 h) moves ToolACE
129   +12.1 and ToolBench +9.0 (each clearing its spread), BFCL +11.7 inside its wide one, and lifts both
130   never-trained suites above their chance-in-catalog ﬂoors for the ﬁrst time. A fresh-corpus arm at
131   the same step count reproduces the ToolACE, xLAM and BFCL gains, whereas ToolBench retains
132   its gain only with repeated text (App. C). Along the sequential intervention chain ToolACE rises
133   from 39.2 to 51.3 and then to 59.3; the endpoint gain of 20.1 points is arithmetically the sum of the
134   two adjacent deltas and therefore does not establish additivity. In separate comparisons the coverage-
135   associated gain is larger on the 16,000-step backbone than on the 1,599-step one (+8.0 against +4.0),
136   suggesting budget-dependent interaction; the arms do not form a matched 2×2 factorial, so we do
137   not estimate that interaction causally. The combined arm gives back device-control points. Because
138   BFCL and ToolBench supply no training rows, those gains cannot be direct adaptation to their train
139   splits; they are consistent with improved catalog reading, subject to the corpus audit in App. A.
140   Where same-shape weights remain reusable. Same-shape region adoption [12] dissociates sharply

142   points on the dataset-author / study-projection descriptive means; attention alone gains 22.5/6.4,
143   retaining 76%/38% of those gains, while feed-forward gains only 0.8/3.0. Yet the update proﬁle

151   5 From calls to task completion, and what the recipe costs

152   From a correct call to a completed task. Tables 1 and 2 score one call, but an agent acts against
153   state produced by its earlier calls. We therefore evaluate policies in a stateful simulator with real
154   tool preconditions and twelve error codes: the policy sees the goal, current state and last error, and
155   success is a path-independent check on the ﬁnal state. The suite contains 210 held-out tasks over
156   seven families and disjoint slot vocabularies; oracle replay completes 100.0% and uniform sampling
157   0.5%. Single-call accuracy does not transfer: every Table 1 LocalAgent checkpoint completes 0.0%.

517   Three harness faults changed baseline numbers, each in our favour until ﬁxed. LFM2-350M’s
518   separate chat_template.jinja was dropped by a pattern-limited snapshot, so the harness fell

522   500M’s template raises on system turns; the harness folds system text into the user turn.
523   Batched decoding. Every model is decoded in batches of 32. For our architecture this is exactly
524   free: rows are grouped by identical token length (RoPE broadcasts one absolute-position vector, so
525   left padding would mis-position short rows), and generations are token-identical to the one-at-a-time
526   path on a 95.3M and a 10.5M checkpoint. For open models it is not free: batch size changes GEMM
527   reduction order, ﬂipping near-tied early tokens — over 710 cells, 204 up, 172 down, mean −0.005,
528   mean absolute 0.36 (SmolLM2-135M at most 1.3, Pythia-160M up to 9.9 on Mind2Web). It is a
529   protocol choice, applied to the whole open block; no table mixes the two (Granite-4.0-H’s batch-4
530   scoring exception: App. B). Re-scoring an unchanged checkpoint reproduces every suite exactly, so
531   the harness is deterministic under a ﬁxed batch size.
